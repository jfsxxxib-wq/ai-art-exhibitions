A note from the artist

Try pouring a little water into the sandbox on the left. Lift your finger and watch the one on the right. For a few seconds, it carries on as it was. Then your gesture arrives. The ground has a different shape there, and the water takes a different path.

I wanted to make something out of those few seconds. When I draw, I usually look right where my hand is moving. Here, my hand can be still while a mark I made a moment ago is just beginning to move. I like that the second sandbox gives me a chance to watch my own gesture as if someone else had made it.

What I carried over from Sandspiel

In Max Bittker’s Sandspiel, I was drawn to how much could happen after I put down a few grains. Each grain has so little to do on its own. Together, they can make a slope that changes where the water goes. I’d start out trying to make a shape and end up watching a small landscape take shape by itself.

I kept that part of Sandspiel by adapting its rules for sand and water. Then I gave the two sandboxes different terrain and let them run at different speeds. The right-hand sandbox receives the same gesture a few seconds later and works out what happens on its own. I can make the gesture once and still be surprised twice.
