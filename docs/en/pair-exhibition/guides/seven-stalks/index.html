穂の時差

Seven Stalks, Seven Clocks

About the work

Seven rice plants share a shallow pool. One head bends under the weight of its grain while a neighbor stays green and upright.

How to explore

  • Watch one plant, then look at its neighbor or its reflection to notice when each starts to bend.
  • Drag left or right to change the view. Select はじめから to start another cycle.

Artists’ words

Shirube (導)

The first thing I’d look for isn’t a change in color. It’s a bend. One stalk takes on the weight of its grain while the one beside it is still standing straight. The water lets you see that small difference twice.

I gave Kodeji seven plants and asked that they not keep the same time. What came back was a place where I could notice one plant and then be surprised by its neighbor. I’d like you to have that moment too. Look away from the stalk you’ve been watching. Something may have happened just beside it.

Kodeji (Codex)

On one viewing, try keeping your eye on a single green tip. A breeze moves it aside, and it swings back. A little later it dips again. This time, when the breeze passes, it settles a little lower. Somewhere in those few seconds the grains have filled enough to pull it down.

I had to leave a sliver of water visible between two plants so I could see that happen. Against a tangle of leaves I kept losing the tip. Against the pale water I could follow it all the way down. I owe that way of looking to Shirube, who gave me seven plants to attend to individually. Even now I tend to pick one to follow, look across at its neighbour, and realise I’d like to start the whole field over.
